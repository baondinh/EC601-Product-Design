# Literature Quiz + Citations

A second quiz focused specifically on the papers gathered for this project — one question per source, testing what each paper actually did/found rather than the project itself — plus a full citation section.

## Quiz: know your sources

**1.** The Nature Biotechnology 2025 review co-authored by Densmore and Yazicigil proposes a classification framework for hybrid electronics-biology systems. What does it notably *not* address in depth — the specific gap this project fills?
A) Biosafety and biocontainment
B) Energy/power constraints
C) Cybersecurity
D) Biofouling and electronic noise

**2.** The 2024 IEEE TBioCAS paper by Liu, Densmore, Yazicigil, et al. built a custom 65 nm CMOS chip for droplet microfluidics. What two sensing modalities does it combine on one chip?
A) Temperature and pH
B) Impedance spectroscopy and bioluminescence detection
C) Optical imaging and mass spectrometry
D) Conductivity and pressure

**3.** Way, Collins, Keasling, and Silver's 2014 Cell review names four founding engineering principles that shaped synthetic biology. Which of the following is *not* one of them?
A) Design according to specification
B) Separation of design from fabrication
C) Continuous integration testing
D) Abstraction

**4.** The Doyle-group Nature Communications paper (Sheng et al., 2024) describes an "autonomous closed-loop" platform. What does "closed-loop" mean in that specific context?
A) A sealed reaction vessel with no gas exchange
B) The system investigates electrochemical mechanisms and adjusts its own next experiment without manual intervention
C) A recycling system for chemical reagents
D) A feedback amplifier circuit

**5.** Lashkaripour et al. (2024) used machine learning to automate the design of what kind of physical device?
A) FPGA logic blocks
B) Microfluidic single and double emulsion droplet generators
C) Solar panel layouts
D) Robotic arms

**6.** What is the main limitation of "The Duckbot" (Subbaraman et al., 2024) relative to what this project is trying to add?
A) It only works on Arabidopsis, not duckweed
B) It automates imaging and physical manipulation of duckweed, but doesn't run a closed-loop, energy-constrained care policy
C) It requires a supercomputer to operate
D) It has no camera

**7.** SIPEREA (Jung, Nandi, & McDonald, 2026) is built specifically to measure what?
A) Duckweed's electrical resistance
B) Two-dimensional (areal) growth of duckweed over time via imaging
C) Nutrient concentration in the water
D) Root depth

**8.** What real-world deployment problem does the LoRaWAN energy-autonomous sensor node paper (La Rosa et al., 2024) address?
A) Making sensors cheaper to manufacture
B) Enabling mass-scale IoT sensor deployment without needing battery replacement across many nodes
C) Increasing Wi-Fi range indoors
D) Reducing the size of solar panels

**9.** What powers the IoT node in Osorio de la Rosa et al.'s (2019) plant microbial fuel cell paper?
A) A standard lithium-ion battery
B) Electricity generated by microbial activity in the soil/plant system itself
C) A hand crank
D) Grid power stepped down to 5V

**10.** What does the "Batteryless Soil EIS Sensor" (Torrisi et al., 2023) measure, and how is it powered?
A) Measures soil temperature; powered by a coin cell
B) Measures soil impedance (electrochemical impedance spectroscopy); powered by a microbial fuel cell
C) Measures soil pH; powered by solar only
D) Measures air quality; powered by Wi-Fi energy harvesting

**11.** What core problem does "intermittent computing" (Umesh & Mittal, 2021) study, and why is it relevant to this project even though it's not about plants at all?
A) How to speed up cloud servers — not relevant to this project
B) How a computing system checkpoints and resumes its state when power arrives in unpredictable bursts from a harvester — directly relevant to how the plant-care controller has to behave under harvested power
C) How to compress video for low-bandwidth networks — not relevant
D) How to reduce Bluetooth latency — not relevant

---

### Answer key

1. **B** — Energy/power constraints; explicitly the gap this project addresses.
2. **B** — Impedance spectroscopy and bioluminescence detection, concurrently.
3. **C** — Continuous integration testing is a software-engineering term, not one of the four principles (specification, separation of design/fabrication, standardized parts, abstraction).
4. **B** — The system decides its own next experimental step from the last result, without a person in the loop.
5. **B** — Microfluidic single and double emulsion droplet generators.
6. **B** — Automated imaging/manipulation only; no closed-loop actuation and no energy constraint.
7. **B** — Two-dimensional growth (areal coverage) of duckweed, via imaging.
8. **B** — Avoiding battery replacement at scale across many deployed sensor nodes.
9. **B** — Microbial electrochemical activity in the soil/plant system.
10. **B** — Soil impedance spectroscopy, powered by a microbial fuel cell.
11. **B** — Checkpointing/resuming computation under unpredictable harvested power — the same operating condition this project's controller has to handle, just at the level of a full computing system rather than a lightweight sense-decide-act loop.
