## What goes in the plan (Should be two pages ... taken from project proposal)
### 1. Mission: 
For growers who need a living plant cared for without reliable power, the **Plant-Care Controller** is a solar-harvested embedded system that only acts when it can afford to — unlike always-powered auto-waterers, which never have to ask that question.
### 2. Target user. 
One specific person. If your product has several users, name the primary one and build for them first. 
### 3. User stories. 
Your top5, with acceptance criteria. Put them on your GitHub board as issues - the board is the plan; the document just explains it. 
### 4. Feasibility 
Show me, don't tell me. "We will use dataset X" is a wish. Downloaded, loaded by a script, committed to the repo - that is feasibility. Same for API keys and hardware: prove access thiss week. 
### 5. Tooling.
Languages, frameworks, models, and why - one line each. Full setup: next slide. 
### 6. The demo sentence. 
"At the end of two weeks we will show ____ working end to end." One sentence. If you cannot write it, your goals are too vague. 

At the end of the two weeks, potentially bring in a moss wall built from the moss grown using the system. Could potentially bring in the system as well. 

### 7. Riskiest Assumption - and its test. 
List 3-5 assumptions your project stands on. Pick the one that kills the project if wrong. Test that one first, with the chepest test youc an find. Write your pivot line now: "We change direction if ..." 

### 8. Evaluation with a Baseline. 
Name your metric AND what you compare against. "Our system finds relevant papers" is marketing. "Beats keyword alerts on recall, on a 50-paper labeled set" is research. No baseline = no claim. If you cannot name a baseline, you have not defined the task yet - that is a finding, write it down. 

### 9. Related work, as a team. 
Each of you read papers for Project 1. Merge them: 8-10 papers, one line each - what it does. 

### 10. 


How you work together: 
Roles. Who owns what for this sprint. Rotate a sprint lead - everyone leads once this semester. 
Cadence. When you meet, where you talk (one channel, not five), and how fast you answer each other. 
Decisions. When two of you dsiagree, how does the team decide? Vote? Sprint lead calls it? Pick one now.
Silence. What happens when someone disappears for three days? Agree on the first step - it is a message to them, not a message to me. 

Put it in the repo as TEAM.md When there is a conflict later - and there will be - your agreement handles it before I do. 

### Two AIs review your plan - make them fight each other 
Step 1. give the same plan to both. Ask each the same three questions: Waht is our riskiest unstated assumption? Why might this fail by week 6? What is missing from our evaluation? 
Step 2. Cross the reviews: give each model the other's critique. Ask it to attack or defend it. 
Step 3. Write the short log: where they agreed (probably a real problem), where they disagreed (decide and say why), and the one change you made to the plan because of it. 

Put chat links in the repo
